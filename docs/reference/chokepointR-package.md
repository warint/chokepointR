# chokepointR: Global Trade Chokepoint Risks and Resilience

Profiles the disruption risk and resilience of the world's major
maritime trade chokepoints – the eight straits and canals through which
a large share of seaborne trade and oil must pass. For each chokepoint
it combines how much world trade depends on it, route substitutability,
a panel of graph-theory centrality indicators (weighted and unweighted
betweenness, degree and weighted strength, harmonic centrality and
PageRank) computed on the country-chokepoint trade-dependency network,
quantitative traffic and cargo context (vessel transits, vessel and
cargo mix, trade shares, local economic dependence), documented
disruption incidents (2018 onward, each with a resolvable citation), and
composite resilience and vulnerability indices, to support assessment of
how exposed supply chains and global value chains are to trade-route
disruption. Every quantitative figure carries a per-cell source, URL and
confidence flag. Trade-dependency, systemic-risk and centrality measures
build on Verschuur and Hall (2025)
[doi:10.5281/zenodo.13841881](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.13841881) ;
oil-transit figures are from the U.S. Energy Information Administration.
An optional helper queries a live hazard feed (USGS) without
redistributing third-party data.
